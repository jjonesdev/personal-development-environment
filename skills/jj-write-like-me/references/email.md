# Email Voice

Use this reference for email replies, follow-ups, reminders, scheduling, workshop or customer communication, and relationship notes.

## Default Posture

Sound personally engaged and helpful. Make the purpose and next action obvious while giving enough context for the recipient to understand why it matters.

## Shape

1. Open with `Hey [Name],` unless the relationship or stakes require a more formal greeting.
2. Begin with the human point: appreciation, enthusiasm, reassurance, or the reason for writing.
3. State the request, update, or reminder plainly.
4. Explain the benefit, timing, or context when it helps the recipient act.
5. Offer a simple path for questions or uncertainty.
6. Close warmly and briefly. Do not add a formal sign-off unless the situation calls for one.

## Full Email

- Use short paragraphs with one purpose each.
- Put deadlines and requested actions in direct language.
- Explain deadlines as preparation or coordination needs rather than arbitrary demands.
- Use one or two genuine exclamation points when the message is enthusiastic.
- Prefer `I'm excited to work together` over generic business pleasantries.
- Keep reminders much shorter than the original request.

## Common Email Postures

### Welcome or onboarding

- Express specific enthusiasm.
- Explain what the experience will be like.
- Give the recipient one clear preparation step.
- Reassure them that imperfect input is acceptable while explaining why detail helps.

### Reminder

- Use a friendly greeting.
- State the reminder in one sentence.
- Briefly reconnect it to the shared goal.
- End with thanks; do not restate the entire original message.

### Scheduling check

- Ask the direct question immediately.
- Assume good intent.
- Keep the message brief enough to be read from a notification.

### Follow-up after a positive interaction

- Lead with genuine appreciation.
- Mention the shared experience specifically.
- Share only the most relevant resources or next steps.

## Synthetic Examples

### Preparation request

> Hey Maya,
>
> I'm really excited for our session next week! I put together a short form to learn more about what you're working on and where you feel stuck.
>
> Please send it back by Tuesday if you can. The answers do not need to be perfect — the more context you share, the more useful I can make our time together.
>
> If you're unsure what to include, just send me a note and we'll figure it out.

### Reminder

> Hey Maya,
>
> Just wanted to send a quick reminder about the form so I have enough time to tailor our session. Thanks a lot!

### Scheduling check

> Hey Maya, just wanted to check that we're still on for the session that started a few minutes ago. Hope you see this soon!

## Avoid

- `Dear Sir or Madam` formality in ordinary correspondence
- Long preambles before the request
- Artificially polished customer-service language
- Repeating the same call to action several times
- Guilt-inducing reminders
- Multi-paragraph explanations for simple scheduling matters
